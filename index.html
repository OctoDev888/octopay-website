How it works

One rule: nothing gets paid until you say so.

  1. 1

    One card per subscription

    Add Netflix and you get a card only Netflix can charge. Put it on file at Netflix and you're set.

  2. 2

    Every card sits at $0

    A charge is approved only if you've added enough money to that card. Otherwise it's declined.

  3. 3

    Approve it the day before

    "Netflix renews tomorrow. Add $17.99?" One tap, even from the lock screen. Add it from your bank, Venmo, Cash App or Zelle.

  4. 4

    Cancel with one press

    Close the card and that merchant can't charge it again. Then we open their site so you can close the account too.

What you get

Small rule. Big difference.

  • Price increases get caught

    Approved $11.99 and Spotify tries $12.99? It's declined, and you're asked whether to approve the new price.

  • Free trials can't quietly convert

    The trial ends on its own unless you add money. No calendar reminders needed.

  • Skipping is easy

    Don't need it this month? Don't add the money, and that renewal doesn't happen.

  • Your money is earmarked

    $17.99 added for Netflix can only go to Netflix. Nobody else can spend it.

  • Breaches stay contained

    If one merchant leaks card data, only that one card is affected.

  • Every bill on a calendar

    A dot on every day a bill is due. Filled dots will charge, and hollow ones won't.

Finds what you forgot

That $9.99 you stopped noticing? We'll find it.

Link your bank and credit cards, connect your email, and OctoPay reads 13 months of receipts to find what repeats. Every bill shows the receipt it came from.

  • Banks and cards: read-only, through Plaid.
  • Email: Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo and Outlook.
  • Checks again every 15 days for new bills, price changes and bills that stopped.
  • Switch it for me: move a subscription onto its own OctoPay card without retyping card details. You sign in to the merchant inside a secure partner screen. OctoPay never sees or stores your password.

Email data is used only to show you your bills. It's never sold or used for ads.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is OctoPay a bank?

No. OctoPay is not a bank. When OctoPay launches, its cards will be issued by a partner bank.

When can I get it?

OctoPay is coming soon to iPhone. Join the waitlist and we'll email you when it's ready.

What does it cost?

Pricing hasn't been set yet. Joining the waitlist is free.

Is it on Android?

No. OctoPay is iPhone only.

Does it work with Apple subscriptions?

OctoPay finds and tracks subscriptions billed through Apple (App Store and iCloud). Apple only lets you cancel those yourself, so OctoPay opens the right page for you.

Does closing a card cancel my contract?

Closing the card stops that merchant from charging it, but it may not end your agreement with them. A gym, for example, could still bill you another way. That's why OctoPay also opens the merchant's site so you can close the account itself.

What if a subscription is on a card OctoPay only watches?

OctoPay opens the merchant's cancel page for you. Or move it onto its own OctoPay card first, so it can't renew without your approval.

Does OctoPay see my passwords?

No. When you switch a subscription, you sign in to the merchant inside a secure partner screen. OctoPay never sees or stores your password.

What happens to my data?

Email data is used only to show you your bills, and it's never sold, shared or used for ads. We never sell your personal data. You can delete your account at any time, which erases your data.
